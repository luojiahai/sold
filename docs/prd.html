The problem

The listings the portals never see

realestate.com.au and Domain have effectively complete coverage of on-market listings. Any product competing there competes on presentation, not on supply.

The listings they don't have are the ones that never reach them:

  • Off-market sales, where a vendor wants discretion and an agent works a private buyer list.
  • Pre-market teasers — "coming soon", "launching next week" — posted to build interest before the portal listing goes live.
  • Private sales and small-agency stock where the portal fee isn't justified.
  • Auction results posted within the hour, days before they appear anywhere structured.

These circulate on Instagram first, because that is where agents build their personal brands. A buyer's agent who sees a Bondi off-market post the morning it goes up has days of advantage over one waiting for a portal listing that may never come.

SOLD finds those posts.

Scope

An instrument, not a product

The product vision serves buyer's agents and active buyers, and the value is time. This prototype serves neither. It is an instrument, and its user is whoever is deciding whether the product is worth building.

It exists to answer five questions that cannot be reasoned out:

  1. 01 Yield. How many genuine Australian listings does a week of harvesting actually produce? Measured as verified posts per term per run.
  2. 02 Precision. What fraction of collected posts survive detection, and what does the noise look like? Split by failure mode: not a listing, versus not Australian.
  3. 03 Uniqueness. Are these listings absent from the portals, or a slower mirror of them? The question the entire product claim rests on.
  4. 04 Cost. What does a post cost to collect and classify? Recorded per run in USD, straight from the detector.
  5. 05 Durability. How long does a burner session survive, and how fast do the endpoints move? Determines whether free collection is viable at all.

Success criterion

The prototype succeeds if it produces a defensible answer to "is there enough unique listing supply on Instagram to build a product on?" — including a well-evidenced no.

Explicit non-goals: deduplication against portal listings, address resolution and geocoding, alerting, saved searches, multi-tenancy, and anything resembling production infrastructure.

Architecture

Two seams, chosen at run time

Three components, two of which are interfaces with multiple implementations. That pluggability isn't speculative generality — it follows from a real constraint: platform access is a moving target with a cost, risk and reliability trade-off, and the right choice will change.

Collector

Takes seed terms and a date range, returns normalised posts. Implemented as a Python sidecar wrapping instagrapi, streaming NDJSON back to Node, so posts land in the run log as they arrive rather than in one lump at the end.

The operational fact that matters most

The two strategies are not equivalent

Neither Instagram surface supports server-side date filtering, and they fail that constraint in completely different ways. Treating them as interchangeable would make every coverage number the system produces a fiction.

This is why keyword_serp reports budget_exhausted and never date_cutoff, however its results happen to fall. Every term in every run records why it stopped:

TerminationMeaningAre the numbers trustworthy?
date_cutoff Walked back past the start date Yes — the window is covered
source_exhausted Instagram stopped returning results Yes — nothing left to fetch
budget_exhausted Hit the page or post cap No — the count is a floor, not a measurement
error Request failed No — partial at best

Without a termination reason, a quiet week and a truncated crawl produce identical numbers.

Detector

Two booleans, not one

Relevance here is a judgement call, which is why detection is AI-powered. A post mentioning property is not a listing, and a listing is not necessarily Australian. The detector answers those as separate questions: isListing and isAustralia.

Collapsing them would destroy the diagnostic signal. A Portland bungalow and a mortgage-broker ad both fail the filter, but for opposite reasons — one is a seed-list problem, the other a prompt problem. From a live run:

PostListingAUVerdict
@jelliscraig_hawthorn — auction, Hawthorn VIC yesyes Verified
@eastside.buyersagent — off-market, Bondi Beach NSW yesyes Verified
@pdxhomes — bungalow, Portland Oregon yesno Wrong country
@someagency — team celebration, hiring post no— Not a listing

The same call extracts listingType, suburb, state, priceText and agency — near-zero marginal cost once the model has read the caption, and the difference between a browsable feed and a list of links.

Verdicts are stored append-only, one row per post per detector per run. Re-running under a different detector adds a verdict rather than replacing one, which is what makes two implementations comparable head to head — the entire reason detection is an interface.

Reliability: batches of ten, three concurrent. A batch that fails to parse retries once, then falls back to per-post calls, so one malformed caption cannot poison nine good ones. A model that silently drops a post fails the batch rather than leaving that post permanently undetected.

Interface

The rejected pile is the product

The feed shows verified Australian listings by default, filterable by state, listing type and free text. Rejected posts are one dropdown away, with their reasons attached.

That is a deliberate decision, not a debug affordance. During validation, what the detector throws away is the more informative half: it is how you see good posts being discarded, or noise the seed list should never have dragged in. The keyword page carries the same logic — per-term yield, so a term that costs requests and returns nothing becomes visible and prunable.

Risk

What could sink this

Terms of use — the central platform risk

Cookie-based collection with burner accounts breaches Instagram's Terms of Use. Meta has litigated against scrapers. The exposure is real, and throwaway accounts do not mitigate it — they only make it cheaper to absorb.

Acceptable for a local prototype deciding whether the product is worth building. Not a foundation for a commercial one. If SOLD proceeds, the vendor-API collector stops being a placeholder and becomes the production path — which is exactly why it sits in the interface from day one.

Platform fragility

Instagram removed the chronological "Recent" hashtag tab from its UI between 2020 and 2023; everything is ranked now. The API parameter requesting recency still exists and is what both gallery-dl and instagrapi send — but whether Instagram still honours it is unverified against a live session, and it is the largest open technical question in the build. If it is ignored, the date-range guarantee degrades to best-effort on both strategies.

Detection blind spot

A significant share of Australian agency posts put the address, price and auction time in the image, with a caption of emojis and hashtags. Text-only detection misses those systematically. The multimodal detector exists to measure that gap: run both over the same posts and compare.

Session mortality

The likeliest way a run dies. Mitigated by a persisted device fingerprint, conservative serial pacing with jitter, preflight before every run, and a hard abort that marks the session expired on a login or challenge redirect rather than limping on.

Prototype-grade execution

Runs execute in-process and die with the server. The run table is the mitigation — an interrupted run is visibly stuck rather than silently absent. A job queue is the production fix and is out of scope.

Roadmap

What happens if the answer is yes

  • Multimodal detector — measure the caption-only recall gap.
  • Vendor-API collector — retire the ToS and session-mortality risks.
  • Portal cross-reference — the uniqueness question, and the actual product claim.
  • Address resolution and geocoding.
  • X and TikTok collectors.
  • Alerting on saved searches — the first thing resembling a product.
