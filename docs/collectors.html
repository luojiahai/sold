How SOLD gets posts off Instagram — three ways to do it

The pipeline

Five steps, one that talks to Instagram

SOLD turns a list of search terms into a feed of verified Australian property listings. The collector is the only step that has to get past Instagram; the other four run on our own machines.

Where we are today

We run option 1 with a single hand-pasted account, no pool, no proxies. One rate-limit stops the whole run. It works, and it is the fragile version of the cheapest option.

The three options

Same input, same output, very different bills

OPTION 1

Burner account pool

Cheapest ToS breach

We own a handful of throwaway Instagram accounts and rotate through them; when one is blocked, the next one takes over automatically.

How it works

  • Log in as a burner account, keep its cookies.
  • Call the same web endpoints the Instagram website uses.
  • Pool of ~10 accounts, each behind its own residential IP.
  • Blocked or challenged → mark dead, pick the next, keep going.

What you get

  • Newest-first hashtag results, so we know when we've caught up.
  • Results within minutes of posting.
  • Everything a logged-in user can see.

What it costs

$10–40 a month. Accounts are free to create, or ~$0.40–$3 each to buy pre-aged (they survive longer); proxy traffic is about 1 GB/month at $4–8/GB. The real cost is engineering time keeping it alive.

What can go wrong

  • Breaches Instagram's Terms of Use. Meta has sued scrapers.
  • Accounts die; expect to replace ~25% a month.
  • Instagram changes an endpoint → we're blind until we patch.
OPTION 2

Third-party vendor API

Lowest risk Pay per post

A scraping vendor runs the accounts, proxies and blocking games; we send a hashtag, they send back posts, we pay per result.

How it works

  • One HTTPS call per search term to the vendor.
  • Vendor returns posts as clean JSON.
  • No accounts, no cookies, no proxies on our side.

What you get

  • Reliability is the vendor's problem, with an SLA.
  • Fastest to build — the adapter is a few days' work.
  • Coverage depends on the vendor's product; "newest first" is not guaranteed everywhere.

What it costs

$1.50–$2.30 per 1,000 posts at Bright Data or Apify — about $75–115/month for us. HikerAPI, a smaller Instagram-only vendor, charges per request and comes in under $50.

What can go wrong

  • Vendor lock-in and price rises.
  • Freshness is theirs to decide — minutes to hours.
  • The vendor is doing the scraping; the legal exposure is theirs, not zero.
OPTION 3

Claude drives a browser

Most expensive ToS breach

A real browser opens Instagram, and Claude reads the page and decides what to click — like a person doing the job, only automated.

How it works

  • Headless browser (Playwright) logged in as one of our accounts.
  • Claude reads the page structure — not screenshots — and extracts each post.
  • Hashtag grids show only thumbnails, so it must open every post.

What you get

  • Survives Instagram changing its private endpoints — it reads what a human sees.
  • Can handle odd layouts and pop-ups without a code change.

What it costs

About 6¢ a post on Sonnet 5 (~2 model calls per post), so roughly $3,000/month at our volume. Haiku 4.5 halves it; screenshot-based control multiplies it 3–5×.

What can go wrong

  • Still needs a logged-in account — inherits every risk of option 1.
  • Slowest by far: seconds per post, not milliseconds.
  • Cost drifts with the page and the prompt; needs constant monitoring.

Read this line

Detection, not collection, is the biggest line for options 1 and 2 — so engineering time and legal risk decide between them, not the bill. Option 3's collection cost alone is more each month than option 2 costs in a year.

Recommendation

Phase it: burner pool now, vendor API for production

We are still proving there is enough supply to build a product on. Spend nothing on collection until that's answered; then pay a vendor to make the risk go away.

Phase 1 · now

Harden option 1

Add the account pool, per-account proxy, and automatic fallback. Finish the first full sweep of all 29 terms.

Exit when: we know posts/day and listings/day for real.

Phase 2 · if supply is real

Switch to option 2

Build the vendor adapter (days, not weeks). Retire the burner accounts. Keep option 1 code as the fallback collector.

Exit when: a paying user depends on the feed.

Option 3

Keep on the shelf

Worth revisiting only if Instagram breaks the web endpoints and no vendor covers what we need. Not before.

Trigger: options 1 and 2 both fail.

Assumptions & sources

  • Volume: 29 seed terms × 60 posts × 1 sweep/day ≈ 1,700 posts/day ≈ 50,000/month.
  • Detection: $1.10 across 105 detections in our database ≈ $0.0104/post, Claude Sonnet 5, prompt v2.
  • Option 1: 10 aged accounts, ~25%/month replaced, residential proxies; ~85 pages/day × ~150 KB ≈ 0.4 GB/month.
  • Option 3: ~2 model calls per post × ~10k input tokens; Sonnet 5 at $2 / $10 per million input / output tokens; Haiku 4.5 at $1 / $5.
  • Apify: Instagram Hashtag Scraper $2.30/1k results on Starter ($29/mo credit, overage pay-as-you-go) — apify.com, pricing.
  • Bright Data: Instagram Web Scraper API $1.50/1k records, no minimum; 5,000 free/month — brightdata.com.
  • HikerAPI: $0.001/request after a $100 deposit ($0.02 on the $20 tier); posts per request not documented, assumed ~27 — hikerapi.com. Range shown covers 1–27 posts per request.
  • Residential proxies: Decodo $3.50–4/GB, IPRoyal $5.50–7.35/GB, Bright Data $8/GB — decodo.com, iproyal.com.
  • Aged accounts: $0.36–$3 bulk auto-registered, $5–20 hand-verified — grey-market vendors, stock quality unverified.
  • Instagram removed logged-out hashtag and keyword browsing in 2024; every option needs a logged-in session somewhere — scrapfly.io (Aug 2026).
  • All prices checked 27 Aug 2026, USD, list price, before tax.
